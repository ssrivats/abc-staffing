How Sarah Chen got hired in four days

A hiring story in six steps — and what happens when the platform remembers everything so the people don't have to.

Role Product Designer
Team Design · Hyderabad
Hiring Manager Sachin Patel
Timeline Oct 22–25

This is the story of one hire. Not the org chart version — the version where a recruiter spots someone on LinkedIn at 9 AM and, four days later, that person has accepted an offer. Every fact verified, every decision attributed, every handoff seamless.

It's also the story of what a hiring platform looks like when it stops thinking in chat threads and starts thinking in hiring cases.

Outreach Engage Schedule Collaborate Interview Decide & Offer
1
Outreach

You find Sarah Chen on LinkedIn. Eight years of product design, currently Senior Designer at Flipkart, IIT Delhi graduate. Her checkout redesign served 200 million users. Her component library is used by 12 teams.

The platform opens a hiring case — not a chat thread, but a persistent record that will follow this candidacy from first message to onboarding. Every fact pulled from her profile lands with its source attached.

Experience 8 yrs product design LinkedIn Current Sr. Designer at Flipkart LinkedIn Education IIT Delhi, B.Des LinkedIn Location Hyderabad LinkedIn

The agent drafts an outreach message from Sarah's profile and the job description. You send it. And then you wait.

What the hiring case captures

Four verified facts, each tagged to LinkedIn. One open question: interest level unknown. No decisions yet. The case is a single source of truth before anything has even happened.

2
Engage

Sarah replies. She's interested — specifically in enterprise design systems work, not consumer products. She asks about the interview process and remote flexibility.

Sarah Chen

Hi! Thanks for reaching out. I've been looking at design systems roles and this sounds interesting. What's the interview process like? And is there flexibility on remote work?

The agent finds answers quietly — not in a loud panel, but as a small gray suggestion inline with the conversation. Hybrid, 2 days a week. Four interview stages, about three hours total. You send the answer and share the job description.

Two pieces of nuance enter the hiring case, both visible to the candidate:

Interest Enterprise design systems, not consumer Interview Candidate Remote Hybrid 2 days/wk is acceptable Interview Candidate

Your first decision is logged: Engage candidate — profile matches requirements. The case knows who decided, when, and why.

3
Schedule

Sarah says she's available Thursday afternoon. The agent checks calendars and suggests 4:00 PM IST — no conflicts. You confirm, and an interview card appears in the conversation with date, time, format, and calendar links.

The stage shifts: Engaged → Interview.

A new fact enters the case: Availability: Thursday afternoons, sourced from the call. A new decision: Schedule Thu 4 PM interview. And a new open question, surfaced by Priya Gupta before she's even spoken to Sarah: B2B enterprise experience depth?

4
Collaborate

This is where most hiring processes break.

The recruiter has context. The hiring manager doesn't. The interviewer has a resume and a calendar invite. The nuance — that Sarah specifically wants enterprise design systems, that her remote question was already answered, that her checkout redesign is the work sample worth probing — lives in the recruiter's head.

In Connect, the handoff is a context packet.

Context Packet — Candidate Briefing
You shared Sarah Chen with the hiring team
Candidate Sarah Chen — 8 yrs product design, Flipkart
Highlight Led checkout redesign (200M+ users), built component library for 12 teams
Interest Specifically looking for enterprise design systems roles
Gap Limited B2B enterprise experience
Interview Scheduled Thu 4 PM — recruiter conversation

Sachin Patel, the hiring manager, responds immediately. He wants to probe the checkout redesign for stakeholder alignment depth. Priya Gupta, the interviewer, flags the B2B enterprise gap as the main risk area.

Both of these become internal nuance — context that shapes the interview plan but is never visible to Sarah.

Sachin Probe checkout redesign for stakeholder alignment Hiring Team Internal Priya B2B enterprise gap is main risk area Hiring Team Internal

Two decisions are logged: Sachin adds the B2B probe to the interview plan. Priya adds enterprise experience questions. The interview guide now reflects the team's actual concerns, not a generic template.

5
Interview

The second context packet fires — this time from you to the full hiring team: Sachin and Priya. It carries everything the team needs to evaluate Sarah, structured as WHAT / WHY / NUANCE / GAP / NEXT.

Context Packet — Recruiter → Hiring Team
Candidate context with Sachin Patel, Priya Gupta
What Sarah Chen — Product Designer candidate, 8 yrs at Flipkart
Why Strong systems thinker. Led checkout redesign for 200M+ users.
Nuance Interested in enterprise design systems specifically, not consumer.
Gap Limited B2B enterprise experience — needs probing.
Next Recruiter conversation Thu 4 PM. Panel to review after.

Sachin's assessment is decisive: the checkout case study was impressive — Sarah managed six stakeholder groups. He's comfortable with the B2B gap. She's a fast learner.

Priya calls it the strongest product thinking exercise she's seen this quarter. She reframed the problem beautifully.

The open questions resolve one by one. B2B gap manageable? Resolved — fast learner per Sachin. Salary within approved band? Resolved — ₹28–32 LPA expectation, band is ₹25–32 LPA.

Decision

Sachin + Priya: Advance to offer — strong scores, B2B gap addressable. Oct 24.

6
Decide & Offer

The final context packet assembles itself — a complete hiring case summary for the offer decision. Candidate, score, strengths, concern, salary, decision. Every source tagged. Every fact traced.

Context Packet — Full Hiring Case Summary
Complete context for offer decision
Candidate Sarah Chen — 8 yrs, Flipkart, IIT Delhi
Score 4.2/5 overall — 3 scorecards, all positive
Strengths Systems thinking, stakeholder mgmt, component library experience
Concern B2B gap — resolved: fast learner per Sachin
Salary Expectation ₹28–32 LPA, band ₹25–32 LPA, offering ₹30 LPA
Decision Offer approved by Sachin Patel

Sachin approves the offer at ₹30 LPA — within the L4 band, matching Sarah's expectation. You send the offer letter and benefits package.

Sarah Chen

This is amazing! I'm thrilled to accept. The team, the role, and the offer all feel right.

Thank you for making this process so smooth — having everything in one conversation made it so easy to stay on top of things.

The hiring case closes. Onboarding triggers automatically: welcome email December 20, IT equipment, team channel, Day-1 orientation January 6. Buddy assigned: Priya Gupta.

The case holds 13 verified facts, 7 pieces of context and nuance, 6 decisions with attribution, and 6 questions — all resolved.
